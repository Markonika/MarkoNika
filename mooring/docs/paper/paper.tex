\begin{abstract}
We describe a C++17 lumped-mass (LM) mooring line solver written from the equations of Paredes (2016), with a seabed contact model, Morison drag and added mass, point elements (clump weights and floaters), a nonlinear/viscoelastic rope law, non-uniform lines, explicit and implicit (error-controlled generalised-$\alpha$) line integration, a small-angle six-degree-of-freedom (6-DOF) rigid body with explicit or implicit coupling of any number of concurrently solved lines, and linearisation about the static state. The code is verified against closed-form solutions (elastic catenary, vibrating string, point-mass oscillators; second-order convergence) and validated against four experimental data sets: the Chalmers 33\,m chain \cite{bergdahl2016}, the Paredes wave-energy-converter buoy with three mooring configurations \cite{paredes2016}, the Azcona et al.\ submerged chain \cite{azcona2017}, and the Lopez-Olocco et al.\ clump-weight chain \cite{lopezolocco2022}. For forced-fairlead chain tests the maximum fairlead tension is reproduced to $2.0\,\%$ on average over 35 clump-free cases and to $0.8\,\%$ and $-1.8\,\%$ over nine cases each with a clump weight; for the Chalmers data set the regression $r^2=0.985$. The study also documents the cases that do \emph{not} agree: a snap-loading case (+29\,\%, not converged under refinement), a buoy pitch period ($-6.2\,\%$), moored-buoy wave responses (heave 10--21\,\% low, surge over-predicted; a computed BEM hull model improves heave and some pitch cases but not surge) and a clump pretension rise that is $3$ percentage points below the reported value. No hydrodynamic coefficient was tuned to any comparison.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Lumped-mass models discretise a mooring line into point masses joined by massless axial springs and dampers. They are the standard tool for line dynamics in floating-platform design, and several implementations exist (for example MoorDyn \cite{hall2015}). Their credibility rests on validation against measurements, which is scarce for small, well-instrumented set-ups where the input data are fully known.

This paper reports a new implementation and a validation campaign built around the principle that every disagreement is reported, no coefficient is tuned after seeing the result, and the quality of the evidence is stated for each comparison (digitised charts, values read by eye from figures, or tabulated data). The contributions are: (i) a complete, documented formulation including the numerical limits that govern the explicit time step; (ii) a verification suite; (iii) four validation comparisons with sensitivity studies; (iv) a candid list of limitations.

The implementation follows the equations of Paredes \cite{paredes2016} (equation numbers below refer to that thesis); no source code from any other mooring solver was used.

\section{Formulation}
\subsection{Line discretisation and tension}
A line of unstretched length $L$ is divided into $N$ segments of length $l_0=L/N$. Node $i$ has position $\vect r_i$ and mass $m_l l_0$ ($m_l$ the dry mass per unit length; half-masses at the ends). With segment stretch $\varepsilon_j=\lvert\vect r_{j+1}-\vect r_j\rvert/l_0-1$ the tension is bilinear with internal damping (Eq.~3.25),
\begin{equation}
T_j=\max\!\bigl(0,\;EA\,\varepsilon_j+c_{\mathrm{int}}\dot\varepsilon_j\bigr),
\end{equation}
so a slack segment carries no load. Stiffness-proportional damping is expressed as $c_{\mathrm{int}}=\beta EA$.

\subsection{Environmental loads}
The submerged weight per unit length (Eq.~3.26) blends the dry and submerged values about the still-water level. Drag on a node uses the relative water velocity $\vect v_r$ with tangential and normal Morison coefficients over the stretched tributary length (Eqs.~3.28--3.31),
\begin{equation}
\vect F_D=\Bigl[\tfrac12\rho D\,C_{dt}\,v_{rt}\lvert v_{rt}\rvert\,\vect t+\tfrac12\rho D\,C_{dn}\,\lvert\vect v_{rn}\rvert\vect v_{rn}\Bigr]\,l_0(1+\varepsilon).
\end{equation}
Added mass acts in the direction normal to the line, $m_a=C_m\rho A(1+\varepsilon)l_0$, and the node acceleration is obtained by solving $\bigl(m\,\mathbf I+m_a(\mathbf I-\vect t\vect t^{\mathsf T})\bigr)\vect a=\vect F$ per node (Eq.~3.27), with the tangent $\vect t$ from the neighbouring nodes. The inverse mass matrix is $\mathbf M^{-1}=\vect t\vect t^{\mathsf T}/m_t+(\mathbf I-\vect t\vect t^{\mathsf T})/m_n$.

\subsection{Waves and currents}
Waves are linear Airy components over constant depth with optional Wheeler stretching. Irregular seas are generated from a JONSWAP or Pierson--Moskowitz spectrum with prescribed $H_s$ and $T_p$: $n$ equal frequency bins, one component at a (seeded) uniformly random frequency in each bin with amplitude $\sqrt{2S(\omega_i)\Delta\omega}$ and random phase, rescaled so that $\sum a_i^2/2=H_s^2/16$ exactly. The first-order load on the body is $F_i(t)=\sum_k w_i(\omega_k)a_k\sin(\omega_kt+\phi_k+\delta_i(\omega_k))$ with a frequency table $w_i,\delta_i$ interpolated linearly (a component outside the table is rejected); the body added mass and damping are constant or, optionally, frequency dependent: with tabulated $\mathbf A(\omega)$, $\mathbf B(\omega)$ from a diffraction solution the body obeys the Cummins equation $(\mathbf M+\mathbf A_\infty)\ddot{\vect\xi}+\int_0^t\mathbf K(t-\tau)\dot{\vect\xi}(\tau)\,\mathrm d\tau+\mathbf C\vect\xi=\vect F$, with the retardation function $\mathbf K(t)=\tfrac2\pi\int_0^\infty\mathbf B(\omega)\cos\omega t\,\mathrm d\omega$ obtained from the piecewise-linear tabulated $\mathbf B$ by exact segment integrals (with an $\omega^{-2}$ tail), $\mathbf A_\infty$ from the table or from the Ogilvie relation, the convolution evaluated by the trapezoid rule over the stored velocity history and the zero-lag part treated implicitly in the velocity-Verlet update. A steady current, uniform or depth-varying (table or power law), is added to the orbital velocity for the lines and point elements; there is no wave--current interaction and no current load on the body.

\subsection{Seabed}
Penetration $p>0$ below the seabed gives a normal spring--damper (Eq.~3.32) and a velocity-ramped Coulomb friction (Eqs.~3.34--3.36),
\begin{equation}
F_n=\bigl[K_sD_1\,p-2\zeta\sqrt{K_sD_1m_l}\,\min(0,v_n)\bigr]l_0,\qquad
\vect F_t=-w_{\mathrm{sub}}\,\mu\min\!\bigl(\lvert\vect v_t\rvert/v_{\lim},1\bigr)\,\hat{\vect v}_t\,l_0 ,
\end{equation}
with $K_s$ the soil stiffness per unit length and diameter, $\zeta$ the damping factor, $\mu$ the friction coefficient and $v_{\lim}$ the ramp speed (a numerical parameter).

\subsection{Point elements}
Clump weights and floaters are attached to a node and contribute weight, buoyancy $\rho gV$, Morison drag (isotropic, or with separate tangential and normal coefficients and areas) and added mass (isotropic, or separate tangential and normal values). The anisotropic option was needed to reproduce the clump model of Lopez-Olocco et al.\ (normal added mass $\rho D^3/3$).

\subsection{Rigid body and coupling}
The platform is a 6-DOF rigid body with constant mass-plus-added-mass matrix $\mathbf A$, damping $\mathbf B$, stiffness $\mathbf C$, an optional quadratic drag, a constant load, and small-angle rotational dynamics (Eq.~3.59). Fairlead positions follow from the exact exponential-map kinematics. Any number of lines are attached to the body; at each body step the fairlead forces and moments of all lines are summed (explicit partitioned coupling). The body uses velocity-Verlet with implicit linear damping and the lines are sub-stepped at their own stable time step. Static equilibrium of the body is found by Newton iteration with a finite-difference Jacobian.

\paragraph{Concurrent line solves.} The lines of a platform are independent objects between coupling steps, so their static solves (inside the equilibrium iterations), start-up evaluation and sub-stepping of every body step can run on a thread pool. The results are bit-identical to the serial run; the gain is bounded by the number of lines (2.25 for the three-leg Paredes CAT case, 55.8\,s to 24.8\,s).

\paragraph{Implicit coupling.} For stiff lines on light bodies the explicit scheme is limited to $\Delta t_{\mathrm{body}}<2/\omega_s$, $\omega_s=\sqrt{K_{\mathrm{line}}/M}$. An optional implicit body integration uses Newmark-$\beta$ for the pose $\vect\xi_{n+1}$ with Newton iterations on $\vect R=\mathbf M\vect a+\mathbf B\vect v+\mathbf D_q|\vect v|\vect v-\vect F_{\mathrm{ext}}(\vect\xi,\text{lines},t_{n+1})$, the approximate Jacobian $\mathbf M/(\beta\Delta t^2)+\gamma\mathbf B/(\beta\Delta t)+\mathbf K$ with the static linearised stiffness $\mathbf K$ (refreshed when convergence is slow), and a re-advance of every line from its saved state at each iteration with the trial fairlead path. It removes the stability limit but costs several line advances per step, so it does not help the validation cases of this paper, which are not stiffness-limited.

\subsection{Linearisation}
About a static state the code provides (i) the natural modes of a line, $\mathbf K\vect\phi=\omega^2\mathbf M\vect\phi$ with $\mathbf K=-\mathbf M\,\partial\mathbf A/\partial\vect r$ taken from the finite-difference Jacobian of the implicit solver (symmetrised, dynamic stiffness including the Maxwell branches), nodal inertia including normal added mass, and modal damping ratios $\zeta_k=\vect\phi_k^{\mathsf T}\mathbf C\vect\phi_k/2\omega_k$; (ii) the 6$\times$6 stiffness of the mooring on the body, $\mathbf K=-\partial\vect G/\partial\vect\xi$ by central differences of the static line solutions, the same Jacobian as in the equilibrium iteration; and (iii) the undamped natural frequencies of the body from $(\mathbf C+\mathbf K_{\mathrm{lines}})\vect x=\omega^2(\mathbf M+\mathbf A)\vect x$. Eigenproblems are solved by the cyclic Jacobi method.

\subsection{Rope law}
Synthetic ropes are described phenomenologically by a static tension curve $T_s(\varepsilon)$ (piecewise linear through the origin) in parallel with $n$ Maxwell branches, each a spring $K_k$ in series with a dashpot of relaxation time $\tau_k$:
\begin{equation}
T=T_s(\varepsilon)+\sum_k K_k(\varepsilon-\alpha_k)+c_{\mathrm{int}}\dot\varepsilon,\qquad \dot\alpha_k=(\varepsilon-\alpha_k)/\tau_k .
\end{equation}
The relaxed stiffness is the curve and the high-rate stiffness adds $\sum_kK_k$, which reproduces a dynamic stiffness above the static one; the complex modulus of a branch is $K\omega^2\tau^2/(1+\omega^2\tau^2)+\mathrm{i}K\omega\tau/(1+\omega^2\tau^2)$. The internal strains $\alpha_k$ are held during a step and advanced exactly for a linearly varying strain. The law has no hysteresis in the static curve, no mean-load dependence of the dynamic stiffness and no creep, and its parameters must come from tests of the rope concerned; \emph{it has not yet been compared with rope measurements}.

\subsection{Bending stiffness}
Bending is represented by the discrete curvature energy $U_b=\sum_i EI_i\lvert\vect t_i-\vect t_{i-1}\rvert^2/(2\bar l_i)$ over the interior nodes, with $\vect t_j$ the unit segment tangents and $\bar l_i$ the mean unstretched length of the two segments, i.e. $\sum\tfrac12EI\kappa^2\bar l$ with $\kappa=\theta/\bar l$. The nodal forces are the exact gradient (a five-node stencil, so the Jacobian of the implicit scheme becomes pentadiagonal in blocks and is solved by a banded LU). The ends are pinned by default; a clamped end (built-in end, bend stiffener, hang-off angle) adds $EI(1-(\vect c\cdot\vect t)^2)/l_0$ for the end segment. The explicit step gains the limit $\Delta t\le C_{\mathrm{cfl}}\,l_0^2\sqrt{m_l/EI}/2$ from the highest bending frequency $4\sqrt{EI/(m_ll_0^4)}$.

\subsection{Non-uniform lines}
A line may consist of sections from anchor to fairlead (for example chain--rope--chain), each with its own length, number of segments and properties (stiffness or rope law, mass, weight, diameters, drag and added-mass coefficients, internal damping). Properties are stored per segment and a node takes half of each adjacent segment, so section boundaries need no special treatment. Uniform lines are reproduced bit for bit.

\subsection{Time integration and stability}
Lines are advanced by classical RK4 (a velocity-Verlet alternative is available). The explicit step is the minimum of three limits: the axial-wave CFL condition $\Delta t\le C_{\mathrm{cfl}}\,l_0/c$, with $c=\sqrt{EA/m_l}$; the internal-damping limit $\Delta t\le C_{\mathrm{cfl}}\,m_ll_0^2/(2c_{\mathrm{int}})$; and the seabed contact oscillator limit based on $\omega^2=K_sD_1/m_l$. The damping limit is what makes the Lopez-Olocco runs slow ($\Delta t\approx10^{-5}$\,s). The initial state is a touchdown catenary relaxed by dynamic relaxation with a fictitious mass.

Two implicit options remove the explicit limits. (i) The internal axial damping can be treated implicitly by a Strang splitting (backward-Euler half steps of the dashpot forces around each explicit step), which lifts the damping limit. (ii) The whole line can be integrated by the generalised-$\alpha$ method (Newmark for $\rho_\infty=1$), with Newton iterations on the step increment, a finite-difference block-tridiagonal Jacobian exploiting the three-node coupling (reused for several steps), a backtracking line search against the kink of the bilinear tension, and halving of the step on failure; if Newton fails at the smallest step an explicit micro-step is taken, never an unconverged implicit iterate. An optional error control uses the Newmark local error $(\beta-\tfrac16)\Delta t^2(\vect a_1-\vect a_0)$ converted to a segment-strain error and scaled by a tolerance times the peak strain, with step rejection and an elementary controller (factor limits 0.2--2, safety 0.9, minimum step $1/1024$ of the maximum).

\section{Validation}
Each comparison states the evidence quality. Acceptance criteria were fixed before the runs where noted.

\subsection{Chalmers 33\,m chain \cite{bergdahl2016}}
A 33\,m chain with a circularly driven fairlead (30 cases, radii $0.075$--$0.2$\,m, periods 1.25--3.5\,s). The measured maxima are digitised chart values with a $\pm5\,\%$ reading error. With 66 segments and the published coefficients, the static top tension is $22.653$\,N ($22.685$\,N at 132 segments) against the published $22.68$\,N. The regression of simulated against measured maxima gives $r^2=0.985$ (published model: $0.98$), RMSE $1.4$\,N, bias $+0.12$\,N; 24 of 30 cases lie within $5\,\%$ and all within $6.7\,\%$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:chalmers}).

\paragraph{Disclosure.} The rotation sense is not stated in the inputs. The first full grid was run counter-clockwise (an arbitrary default) and over-predicted by $9.8\,\%$ ($r^2$ against 1:1 $=0.81$). The clockwise sense was then adopted after Fig.~3.5 of the thesis was consulted; the check was motivated by the mismatch. The result therefore depends on this inferred set-up detail. Smooth cases converge for $N\ge33$ (variation $\le0.15\,\%$); the snap case ($r=0.2$\,m, $T=1.25$\,s) scatters by $\pm1.2\,\%$ in $N$.

\paragraph{Regular waves ($T=1.3$ and $1.4$\,s).} With the body coefficients of the thesis (Table~3.4) and Wheeler stretching on the lines, heave is $10$--$21\,\%$ low in 6/6 cases (all within the 25\,\% criterion fixed in advance), pitch meets the 30\,\% criterion in 3/6 (CAT $35$--$50\,\%$ low), and surge is over-predicted by $14\,\%$ up to a factor three. The measured surge RAO has a sharp minimum near $T=1.3$\,s that neither the moored model nor the free hull reproduces. The wave module itself is verified to $10^{-6}$--$10^{-12}$ against closed forms. Submerging the line nodes and point elements at the instantaneous free surface instead of the still-water level changes the responses by at most $1.2\,\%$, so that approximation is not the cause of the disagreements. We regard the wave response as \emph{not validated}.

\paragraph{Wave responses with computed (BEM) hull coefficients.} The measured hydrodynamic data of the experiment are not available to us (the data hosts were unreachable), so the thesis' Table~3.4 could not be checked directly. We therefore computed the hull coefficients ourselves with the open-source boundary-element code Capytaine \cite{ancellin2019} \todo{verify reference}: a vertical cylinder reconstructed from the stated hydrostatics ($D=0.515$\,m, draft $0.176$\,m, centre of gravity $0.100$\,m below the waterline, depth $0.9$\,m). \emph{These are computed, not measured, values; the hull shape is a reconstruction, no viscous effects are included, and nothing was tuned.} Excitation is the sum of Froude--Krylov and diffraction forces. Compared with Table~3.4 at $T=1.3$\,s, surge and heave excitation agree to about $5\,\%$ in magnitude and $0.01$\,rad in phase and the added mass and damping to $6$--$15\,\%$. The thesis pitch moment, however, matches the BEM moment only when the latter is referred to the \emph{waterline} (phase $-1.674$ against $-1.667$\,rad; magnitude $12$--$15\,\%$ low), not to the centre of gravity about which our pitch degree of freedom is defined, so Table~3.4 probably refers to a different reference point. The coupling terms $A_{15}$, $B_{15}$ agree in magnitude to $8$--$17\,\%$ after the shift, but with the opposite sign, which is unresolved. Mesh refinement from about $10^3$ to $3.9\times10^3$ wetted panels changes the excitation by less than $1\,\%$, the added mass and damping by up to $3\,\%$ and the moored RAOs by at most $0.6\,\%$ (CAT).

With the BEM coefficients heave rises by $8$--$13\,\%$ and moves into the measured band for CON1 and CON2 at $1.3$\,s (and CON2 at $1.4$\,s), and the pitch of CON1 and CAT moves towards the data (CAT $35$--$50\,\%$ low $\to$ $10$--$20\,\%$ low). However, the pitch of CON2 was already too high and moves further away ($20$--$38\,\%$ above the measured band at $1.3$\,s), and surge, already over-predicted, becomes worse (about a factor two to three at $1.3$\,s). The CON2 excess was traced with diagnostic runs (not calibrations): the quasi-static mooring stiffness reproduces the CON1 pitch to $1\,\%$ but predicts $2.05$ for CON2 against $2.35$ in the time domain; removing the clump gives exactly the CON1 value ($1.96$), whereas removing the clump drag, doubling the segment count or changing nothing else leaves it unchanged ($2.37$, $2.34$). The linearised line modes of the CON2 leg ($1.33$ and $1.50$\,s, against $0.87$\,s for CON1) suggested a clump--rope resonance, but a period sweep from $1.20$ to $1.50$\,s does not support it: the CON2 pitch falls monotonically with period and the ratio of the time-domain to the quasi-static response rises smoothly toward short periods ($1.07$ at $1.5$\,s, $1.13$ at $1.33$\,s, $1.30$ at $1.2$\,s) with no peak near $1.33$\,s, while the CON1 control stays within $1\,\%$ of its quasi-static value at every period. A frequency-independent reduction of the CON2 pitch stiffness of about $1.5$\,N\,m/rad ($4\,\%$) reproduces the whole sweep (a diagnostic fit, not used elsewhere). A wave-height test at $T=1.3$\,s shows that the excess is nonlinear: the CON2 pitch$/(ka)$ is $2.13$, $2.29$, $2.35$ and $2.33$ for $H=0.02$, $0.04$, $0.08$ and $0.16$\,m (quasi-static value $2.05$), whereas CON1 is independent of $H$ ($1.95$ and $1.96$ at $0.04$ and $0.08$\,m). The CON2 excess is therefore an amplitude-dependent effect of the clump leg. A tension time series excludes loss of tension: in the analysis window no segment of any leg is slack (smallest CON2 segment tension $5.6$\,N against about $6.3$ to $6.8$\,N at rest; CON1 $2.62$\,N), the pitch amplitude is stationary from the fourth cycle on, and the amplitude of the fairlead tension is nearly linear in the wave height ($0.36$\,N at $H=0.02$\,m, $1.29$\,N at $0.08$\,m). The only configuration-specific feature found is that CON2 carries four times the pretension of CON1 ($11$ against $2.7$\,N per leg) at pitch amplitudes of $0.23$\,rad ($13^\circ$), which suggests a geometric large-rotation effect that the small-angle body model cannot represent; we have not tested this, and the mechanism remains unidentified. Separately, the free-hull pitch response sits on a resonance flank (natural period $1.11$\,s, damping ratio $1.3\,\%$) and changes from $2.1$ to $3.0$ for a $15\,\%$ larger pitch inertia, so the absolute pitch level is highly sensitive to the poorly known pitch inertia and the missing viscous damping. The reference-point explanation therefore removes part of the CON1 and CAT pitch deficit but does not make the model consistent with the experiment, and the surge disagreement is not explained by linear potential-flow coefficients. Runs at $T=1.5$\,s have no measured counterpart. The wave response remains \emph{not validated}.

\section{Discussion}
\paragraph{What the evidence supports.} For taut-to-snapping chains driven at the fairlead, the model reproduces maximum fairlead tension to within a few percent for most cases across three independent experiments, with coefficients taken from the sources. Static properties of a three-leg buoy mooring agree within the stated measurement uncertainty.

\paragraph{What it does not support.} (i) Snap loading with total loss of tension is reproduced only to about $+12\pm5\,\%$ even when the time integration is converged; the residual is dominated by the spatial discretisation and by inputs the source does not give. (ii) Wave-driven motions of the moored buoy are not reproduced (surge in particular). Replacing the thesis' hull coefficients by our own BEM solution (computed, not measured) improves heave and the CON1/CAT pitch but worsens the CON2 pitch and the surge, so the hydrodynamic coefficient set is only a partial cause; viscous and second-order effects, the true hull shape and the mooring compliance remain untested. (iii) The coverage is limited to chains and one polyester rope at laboratory scale; no full-scale, multi-line platform or synthetic-rope data have been compared. The nonlinear/viscoelastic rope law and the non-uniform line formulation are implemented and verified against closed forms but have not been compared with rope measurements.

\paragraph{Evidence quality.} Several reference values are digitised or read by eye (Chalmers chart $\pm5\,\%$; Azcona figures $\pm0.5$\,N; Paredes figures), which limits the discrimination of small model errors; the Chalmers rotation sense was inferred after a mismatch; two interpretation choices (Azcona amplitude, Lopez-Olocco clump drag) were required.

\section{Limitations}
Cable without torsion or VIV; small-angle rigid-body dynamics with constant $\mathbf A,\mathbf B$; Euler--Bernoulli bending without torsion, moment--curvature nonlinearity or bending damping; linear waves only (regular or irregular, with no second-order loads, spreading or wave--current interaction; the frequency-dependent hydrodynamics are verified against an analytic kernel, not against measurements, and require the explicit coupling), with line submergence evaluated at the still-water level by default; flat seabed with a spring--damper; explicit coupling whose body step is limited to about $0.5$--$0.9$ of $2/\omega_s$ (an implicit option exists but is more expensive per step and its accuracy at large body steps is limited by the line's own lowest vibration period); linearisation at a single static state without hydrodynamic damping of the line modes; a single rigid body; piecewise-uniform (not continuously tapered) line properties; a heuristic error estimate for the implicit integrator that is relative to the peak strain.

\section{Conclusions and outlook}
A newly written lumped-mass solver with a coupled 6-DOF body reproduces fairlead tension in three chain experiments to a few percent without tuning (clump-free chain $+2.0\,\%$ on average over 35 cases, with clump weights $+0.8\,\%$ and $-1.8\,\%$), and the paper documents where it fails. The implicit and error-controlled integration shows that the time step is not responsible for the snap-case disagreement. Planned work: validation against the NREL/NLR 1:100 mooring data and the OC5 data, which would provide multi-line and larger-scale evidence and the first test of the rope law; second-order wave loads, current loads on the body and measured diffraction data (our BEM hull is computed, with a reconstructed shape) for the validation buoys; and torsion and a nonlinear moment--curvature law for dynamic power cables. \todo{revise once additional experimental data have been compared}
